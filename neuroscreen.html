Owlet: Mobile Neurological Screening Platform

Product / UX Designer

Problem · Why this matters

Early identification can significantly improve developmental outcomes, yet current screening methods are slow, subjective, and inaccessible—especially for families without access to specialists.

At the same time, research shows that eye-tracking and AI can detect early neurological signals with high accuracy, but these tools remain confined to clinical settings.

Owlet bridges this gap by translating clinically validated methods into an accessible, scalable mobile experience—enabling earlier, more objective screening for all families.

Research insights

  • Early detection improves outcomes. Children benefit significantly from earlier diagnosis and intervention.
  • Current screening is unreliable and inaccessible. Existing tools are subjective, inconsistent, and often unavailable to families without specialist access.
  • Technology can do better—but isn’t widely available. Advances in eye-tracking and AI enable accurate early detection, but are still limited to clinical environments.

Solution · What we’re building

Owlet is designed as an end-to-end screening experience—not a loose demo—where capture, interpretation, and handoff are intentional.

  • At-home screening for caregivers. Guided sessions walk families through standardized interactions suitable for infants and toddlers.
  • Multi-modal capture. Eye tracking via the device camera (computer vision) pairs with physiological signals—ECG and heart rate—to enrich behavioral observations.
  • Processing and synthesis. Signals feed a structured pipeline oriented toward consistency and auditability rather than one-off snapshots.
  • Clinician-facing outputs. Results are sent for clinical review: what was measured, under what conditions, and what it suggests for next steps.

Key Features

Guided Screening Flow

Step-by-step sessions that standardize context and reduce variability between at-home runs.

Eye Tracking via Camera

Computer-vision–based attention and gaze patterns to support developmental screening hypotheses.

Physiological Data (ECG + Heart Rate)

Integrates biosignals alongside behavior to ground observations in measurable physiology.

Clinician Reporting & Dashboard

Exports and summaries aimed at specialists—emphasizing clarity, provenance, and appropriate uncertainty.

Caregiver Feedback & Next Steps

Readable guidance for families after a session: what happened, what it may mean, and what to do next clinically.

Design Challenges

Depth of thinking matters more than a roadmap checklist—these tensions shaped the direction of the product.

  • Designing for infants—the indirect user. Every screen must work for caregivers while encoding behaviors the system needs from a child who cannot follow instructions.
  • Caregiver usability vs. clinical rigor. The experience must feel achievable at home without diluting protocols to the point of useless data.
  • Sensitive health data by default. Minimizing collection, surfacing consent and control, and avoiding fear-based language when families are already stressed.
  • Trust in AI- and CV-driven results. Explaining methodology, confidence, and human-in-the-loop review so “algorithmic” does not read as opaque or oracular.

Key Decisions & Tradeoffs

Explicit tradeoffs recruiters and hiring managers look for—documented here the same way as on my shipped work.

Simplicity vs. data accuracy Fewer steps reduce drop-off but can skip calibration that improves signal. The product bias is toward fewer, higher-quality captures over rushed completions.
Real-time feedback vs. processing reliability Live cues can reassure caregivers; heavy real-time computation risks instability on consumer hardware. Owlet favors dependable capture first, then progressive disclosure of status.
Clinical detail vs. caregiver readability Clinicians need parameters and context; families need empathy and clarity. The UI separates expert layers while keeping a single coherent story.
Speed to insight vs. validation burden Early development prioritizes traceability and ethical guardrails over promising speed we cannot yet defend empirically.

User Flows & Screens

Two journeys anchor the system—each labeled so reviewers can map narrative to UI.

Caregiver journey

  1. Onboarding & consent — device permissions, sensor pairing, plain-language study of what data is used.
  2. Guided session — scripted activities optimized for eye tracking and physiological recording.
  3. Completion & summary — caregiver-facing recap with supportive tone and explicit next-step guidance.

Clinician journey

  1. Receive structured report — patient context, session metadata, and fused behavioral + physiological highlights.
  2. Interpret & annotate — drill into modalities, notes, and comparison over time where available.
  3. Decide next actions — referral, watchful waiting, or repeat screening with clearer baselines.
